An order has a **model** (what kind of commerce it is) and a **channel** (where it came in). Both
are part of the order input and both are checked when the order starts. The model also decides which
[flow](/guides/templates/) a new flow starts from and how lines are priced.

## Models

Models: <Values of="order-models" />.

<ModelTable />

`input.model` is required. A flow has one `model` and a list of `channels` it is meant for. Inbound
routing and the UI use the channels; they do not restrict which orders run on the flow.

## Channels

Channels: <Values of="order-channels" />. `input.channel` defaults to `api`. The dashboard starts
orders as `manual`, [email intake](/guides/email-intake/) as `email`, and the daily stock review as
`schedule`. Orders placed by an AI shopping platform through [agentic checkout](/reference/ucp/) arrive as `agent`, labelled AI agent. Storefronts, marketplaces and POS systems send orders with a signed webhook; see
[Inbound orders](/guides/inbound-orders/), and Shopify webhooks and EDI X12 850 purchase orders (channel `edi`, label EDI, `b2b`) arrive through [Channel adapters](/guides/channel-adapters/). Orders on `pos` run the point-of-sale flow, `tpl-pos-sale`, for the `b2c` and `d2c` models. `GET /api/v1/orders` filters on `model` and `channel`
(see [REST API](/reference/rest-api/#list-orders)).

## Pricing

Procurement and replenishment orders are **buy-side**: the business is the buyer, so lines price at
the product's cost. Every other model is **sell-side** and runs the
[pricing chain](/reference/expressions/#pricing-chain) (price book, contract, volume, list).

The [Price](/reference/nodes/#commerce.price) node's `basis` field makes that explicit:

| `basis` | Lines are priced |
|---|---|
| `auto` (default) | At cost for buy-side models, through the chain otherwise |
| `chain` | Through the pricing chain, whatever the model |
| `cost` | At the product's cost |

A line with its own `unitPrice` keeps it under every basis.

## Order fields

Besides `customerId`, `currency`, `lines` and `meta`, the input takes:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `externalId` | The order's id in its channel (storefront, marketplace, POS). Searchable with `q` |
| `contact` | `{ email, name?, phone? }` for a buyer without a customer account, such as guest checkout or a POS receipt. `email` is searchable with `q` |
| `fulfillment` | `{ method, locationId? }`. `method` is one of <Values of="fulfillment-methods" />; `locationId` is the store to fulfil from or hand over at. See [Fulfillment](/guides/fulfillment/) |
| `lines[].sellerId` | The marketplace seller for a line; otherwise the product's seller, or first party |
| `lines[].locationId` | Set by route fulfillment on a split shipment: the store a line ships from. Unset means `fulfillment.locationId`, or central stock |

All of these are on the run context's `order` (see [Expressions](/reference/expressions/#the-run-context)),
so a condition can branch on `order.model`, `order.channel` or `order.fulfillment.method`. The
order list filters on `model` and `channel` and searches `externalId` and the contact email, and the
[outbound webhook](/guides/outbound-webhooks/) order summary carries `model`, `channel` and
`externalId`.

Customers have a `kind`: `business`, `consumer` or `internal`.

## Guest checkout

An order with a `contact` and no `customerId` is a guest order. On the **New order** form, choose
**Guest** under **Buyer** and give an email (and optionally a name), and the **Guest checkout** demo scenario (where the demo data is loaded) starts one.
The order page shows `Guest · <email>` in place of a customer.

- [Credit check](/reference/nodes/#commerce.credit_check): a guest, or a customer whose `kind` is not
  `business`, has no credit account. The node takes the branch set by `noAccount` (`over_limit` by
  default, `ok` to let them through) and reports "Guest checkout (no credit account)" or
  "No credit account (consumer)". A business customer's order is held against their
  [credit account](#credit-accounts).
- [Generate invoice](/reference/nodes/#commerce.invoice): only an invoice to a business customer that is not
  yet paid in full is added to their balance, and its summary ends "· on account".
  Set `onAccount` to `false` to never add it. `terms` defaults to `due_on_receipt`. With
  `provider: stripe` the invoice is also issued through Stripe and paid online (see
  [Payments](/guides/payments/#invoices-paid-through-stripe)); a paid invoice takes its charge back
  off the balance.
- [Notify](/reference/nodes/#commerce.notify) with `to: customer` falls back to `contact.email`.
- [Risk score](/guides/ai/#ai-nodes): the offline heuristic adds 15 for a guest ("Guest checkout")
  and 30 for an order with neither customer nor contact ("Unknown customer").
- The Commerce page's customers table shows each customer's `kind`; a consumer or internal customer
  reads "Pays at checkout" in place of payment terms.

## Credit accounts

A business customer buys on account up to a **credit limit**. What they can still order is the limit
less what they owe (the **balance**, which moves when an order is invoiced) and less what their open
orders have **held**: **available = limit − balance − held**. All of it is in USD.

The [credit check](/reference/nodes/#commerce.credit_check) is also the hold. When an order passes, its
total is claimed from the limit in the same step that checks it, so two orders checked at the same
time can't both pass and together exceed the limit: exactly one takes `ok` and the other takes
`over_limit`. Set the node's `hold` to `false` to only compare, as the check did before holds. A hold
ends one of three ways (see [Credit hold](/reference/lifecycles/#credit-hold) for its states):

- **Charged.** The [invoice](/reference/nodes/#commerce.invoice) converts the order's hold into its charge
  on the balance, so the same credit is never counted twice.
- **Released.** A rollback releases it, as do cancelling the order and every end of the order that
  doesn't invoice it (declined, cancelled or completed without an invoice). Releasing twice does nothing.
- **Expired.** A hold nothing gave back stops counting after the node's `holdFor` (30 days by default).

An order that goes to an approval over its limit holds nothing until it passes a check, and a person's
approval lets it go on without one. Releasing and expiring never move the balance.

A new business customer starts with a limit of zero, so its orders on account take `over_limit` until an
admin sets one. A company that is not active (pending, suspended, rejected or closed) takes no credit:
the check refuses to hold, and its limit can't be changed until the company is reinstated. Recording a
payment or a memo still works.

The customer's page has a **Credit** section with the limit, balance, held and available, and the
ledger behind the balance, newest first with the balance after each entry: invoices, payments,
reversals and adjustments. An admin can **set the limit** or **adjust the balance** (a credit memo or a
payment received outside CommerceWorker lowers what is owed; a debit raises it). Each stops at the
assistant's confirm card, which shows the account now and after, and an adjustment needs a reason. An
adjustment is an entry that is never edited: undo one with another. The same reads and changes are
`GET /api/v1/customers/:customerId/credit`, `POST …/credit/limit` and `POST …/credit/adjustments` over
[REST](/reference/rest-api/#workspace-operations), and `get_credit_account`, `set_credit_limit` and
`adjust_credit` over [MCP](/reference/mcp/#credit-accounts).

## Starting a flow for a model

**New flow** on `/flows` takes a model. With the Template start it copies that model's default flow
from the table above into a new flow of its own, so editing the copy never changes the built-in one
(see [Building Order Flows](/guides/building-flows/#creating-a-flow)). `b2c` starts from the storefront flow, `tpl-storefront-order`, and `d2c` from `tpl-omnichannel-order`, which routes each order to
shipping, store pickup or digital delivery (see [Fulfillment](/guides/fulfillment/)); `marketplace` has its own,
`tpl-marketplace-order` (see [Marketplace](/guides/marketplace/)).
