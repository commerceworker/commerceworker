---
title: Promotions
description: Promotions, with a code or automatic, that take a percentage or an amount off, make shipping free, or give units free or discounted when others are bought, with limits on who can use them and how often, a cap, a priority and rules for combining, applied by a flow's Apply promotions step.
---

A promotion is either a code, such as `WELCOME15`, or automatic: it has no code and applies to
any order it fits. An order carries its codes in `meta.promoCodes` (set by whoever starts it, for
example through the REST API); when it runs, its flow's
[Apply promotions](/reference/nodes/#commerce.discount) step takes the order's codes and every
automatic promotion as candidates, checks each, and applies the ones that win (see
[Combining promotions](#combining-promotions)).

## Creating promotions

Operators manage promotions on **Promotions** (linked from the Pricing section of **Commerce**);
**New promotion** opens the form for a new one. Adding it, and **Pause** or **Resume**, ask on the
[assistant](/guides/assistant/#edits-from-a-page)'s confirm card. The card says what the promotion
takes off, how it combines, who and what it's limited to, and when it ends. A code
is letters, digits, dashes and underscores, up to 32, and is matched however it's typed
(` welcome15 ` is `WELCOME15`). Leave the code empty and the promotion is automatic; it then
needs a name, which is what the page and a buyer's price breakdown call it. Each promotion takes
off one of:

| Takes off | What changes |
|---|---|
| **A percentage** | The prices of the lines it covers, by that percentage |
| **An amount** | That amount, spread over the lines it covers in proportion to their value, never more than they are worth. In the base currency, converted at the order's rate |
| **Shipping** | The order's shipping charge, to zero |
| **Buy X, get Y** | The cheapest units it gives, off the lines that hold them (see [Buy X, get Y](#buy-x-get-y)) |

and can be limited to:

- **Only these SKUs**: it covers only those lines (a buy X, get Y promotion names its SKUs in its own rule instead).
- **Only this customer**.
- **A minimum subtotal**, in the base currency.
- **Uses in all** and **uses per customer**: each order that keeps a promotion uses it once, an
  automatic one included.
- **An end date**.
- **A condition**: a JSON Logic expression the order must also meet (see [Conditions](#conditions)).
- **A cap**: the most it takes off one order, in the base currency, whatever its percentage or
  amount works out to. A free-shipping promotion with a cap takes off at most the cap.

and set apart by how it combines:

- **Combines**: *Never with another* (exclusive, the default) or *With stackable ones*.
- **Priority**: a whole number, higher first (default 0).

**Pause** stops a promotion applying to new orders; **Resume** lets it apply again. One past its
end date is marked **Ended**, paused or not, and offers neither, since neither would change
anything. Its dates can't be edited: to run the offer again, add a new promotion. One for a single
customer names them, linked to their page. The **Used** column counts the orders that have used it.

## Buy X, get Y

A buy X, get Y promotion gives units away, or at a discount, when the order holds enough of
others: "Buy 2 TEE, get 1 free", or "buy 3 of anything, get 1 at half price". Choose **Buy X, get Y**
under **Takes off**; the form then asks for:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Buy** (units) | X, a whole number from 1 |
| **Buy these SKUs** | The lines that count toward buying. Empty is any line |
| **Get** (units) | Y, a whole number from 1: how many units each purchase of X earns |
| **Get these SKUs** | The lines whose units are discounted. Empty is the same lines as the ones bought |
| **Percent off** | What the units it gives lose: 100, the default when empty, is free |
| **Times per order** | How often it applies to one order. Empty is as often as the order allows |

Every other field (minimum subtotal, customer, dates, uses, a condition, a cap, priority and
combining) works as for the other kinds; **Only these SKUs** doesn't, since the rule names its own.
`create_promotion` takes the same as `kind: "bxgy"` with a `bxgy` object, over MCP and REST alike, and
refuses one that can never apply: a quantity below 1 or not whole, a percentage of 0 or over 100,
an empty or repeated SKU list, or a rule on another kind. Its confirm card says the rule in words
("Buy 2 TEE, get 1 free").

```json
{ "kind": "bxgy", "name": "Tee deal", "stacking": "stackable",
  "bxgy": { "buy": { "skus": ["TEE"], "qty": 2 }, "get": { "qty": 1 } } }
```

Buy 2 TEE, get 1 TEE free: every third tee, the cheapest. With `"get": { "skus": ["HAT"], "qty": 1,
"percent": 50 }` it is instead buy 2 TEE, get 1 HAT at half price. With `"repeat": 1` it applies
once however many are bought.

How it works out what to take off:

- **Units, not lines.** A line of 3 is 3 units. Only whole units count (2.5 is 2), and a unit with
  no price or a price of 0 is neither bought nor discounted, so a free gift never earns a discount.
- **The cheapest units are discounted first.** Of the lines that can be discounted it takes the
  cheapest units, each by its percentage. (The surveyed design this came from spread the discount
  over the whole subtotal without picking units; this picks them, so what a buyer sees on each line
  is what they were given.)
- **A unit counts once.** Each time the promotion applies it uses X units to buy and Y to discount,
  and no unit is both. With the same SKUs on both sides, buy 2 get 1 needs three units a time:
  six units earn two free, five earn one, two earn none. When some units could be either, they are
  discounted only while enough are left to buy with, and the order isn't given the promotion until
  it has at least X to buy and Y to get.
- **How many times** is as many as the units allow, up to **Times per order**.
- **Rounding.** The discount on each line is rounded to the cent, and the line carries it as a lower
  unit price (the average over its units, to four decimals), as every promotion does. So the order's
  subtotal, tax, invoices and refunds all work from it. A line of 3 at 10.00 with one unit free
  shows a unit price of 6.6667 and still totals 20.00.
- **A cap** (the most it takes off one order) scales the discount down across its lines.
- **Combining.** It is chosen and ranked like any promotion, on what it would take off alone. In a
  stack it takes its units from the prices the promotions before it left, so a stackable 10% first
  and then buy 2 get 1 free makes the third unit free at its price after the 10%. A line already
  averaged by an earlier buy X, get Y promotion is a single price to a later one.

An order too small for it is told what it needs (`needs 2 TEE and 1 HAT in the order`) if it
entered the code; an automatic promotion that doesn't fit is dropped without a word. Where the
order is shown the line carries the discount: the cart and the order show the discounted unit price
and total (the cart also shows what was taken off the line), and a UCP checkout shows each line's list price and its discount, with the allocation
on that line (see [UCP](/reference/ucp/)).

## Conditions

A condition is a [JSON Logic](/reference/expressions/#json-logic) expression, the same language a
flow's Condition step uses, stored with the promotion. It sits beside the fields above, not
instead of them: a promotion applies only when its limits and its condition both hold. Use the
fields for what they cover (SKUs, one customer, a minimum subtotal, dates) and a condition for the
rest. The form's **Condition** field takes the expression as JSON; **Told to a buyer whose order
doesn't meet it** is the message for a code whose condition fails (it needs a condition).
`create_promotion` takes `condition` and `conditionMessage`, over MCP and REST alike; its confirm
card shows the condition as written.

A condition reads this data, with the names a flow's expressions use:

| Variable | Holds |
|---|---|
| `now` | When the order is priced, in epoch milliseconds |
| `order.channel` | Where the order came in: `web`, `pos`, `api`, `agent`, … |
| `order.currency` | The order's currency |
| `order.subtotal` | The lines' total in the order's currency, before shipping and tax |
| `order.baseSubtotal` | The same subtotal in the base currency |
| `order.customerId` | The buyer's customer id, or `null` for a guest |
| `order.lines` | The lines, each with `sku`, `name`, `qty` and `unitPrice` (`null` when unset); `order.lines.0.sku` reads one by position |
| `order.meta.shipTo.country` | The ship-to country as a two-letter code (`"usa"` reads as `US`), or `null` |
| `order.meta.shipTo.region` | The ship-to state or province code, or `null` |
| `customer.id`, `customer.kind`, `customer.tier` | The buyer's id, kind (`business`, `consumer`, `internal`) and pricing tier; all `null` for a guest |
| `company.status`, `company.tier` | The status (`pending`, `active`, `suspended`, `rejected`, `closed`) and pricing tier of the buyer's [company](/guides/companies/); `null` for a customer with none |
| `buyer.role`, `buyer.team`, `buyer.orderLimit` | The role (`buyer`, `approver`, `company_admin`), team id and own order limit of the member named in `meta.buyerId`; `null` when the order names none |

The subtotal is the order's lines as they stand when the step runs, before any promotion. The data
is read-only: a condition can't change the order. Products' tags and categories aren't available
yet.

Beside `var`, a condition may use `if`, `==`, `===`, `!=`, `!==`, `!`, `!!`, `and`, `or`, `>`,
`>=`, `<`, `<=`, `max`, `min`, `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%`, `in`, `cat`, `substr`, `merge`, `map`,
`filter`, `reduce`, `all`, `some`, `none`, and CommerceWorker's `days` and `len`. Inside `map`,
`filter`, `all`, `some`, `none` and `reduce` only the item is in scope: for a line, `sku`, `name`,
`qty` and `unitPrice` (`current` and `accumulator` in `reduce`). The form and `create_promotion`
refuse, with the reason, an expression that names another variable, uses another operator
(`method`, or the clock's `{"now": []}`: read the variable `now`), computes a variable's name,
nests list operations more than two deep, or runs past 4,000 characters. A condition that can't
be evaluated, or whose result is false, doesn't qualify the order.

Examples:

```json
{ "==": [{ "var": "order.meta.shipTo.country" }, "US"] }
```

Only orders shipping to the US.

```json
{ "and": [
  { "==": [{ "var": "customer.kind" }, "business"] },
  { "in": [{ "var": "customer.tier" }, ["gold", "silver"]] }
] }
```

Business customers in the gold or silver tier.

```json
{ "some": [{ "var": "order.lines" },
  { "and": [{ "==": [{ "var": "sku" }, "WID-100"] }, { ">=": [{ "var": "qty" }, 3] }] }
] }
```

An order with three or more of `WID-100`.

```json
{ "and": [
  { "==": [{ "var": "order.channel" }, "pos"] },
  { "<": [{ "var": "now" }, 1798761600000] }
] }
```

In-store orders until the end of 2026.

A buyer entering a code whose condition fails is told the message you wrote, or
`This order doesn't qualify for WELCOME15.` An automatic promotion whose condition fails is
dropped without a word, like any other automatic promotion that doesn't fit.

## In a flow

The built-in Storefront, Omnichannel and B2B order flows already have a **Promotions** step in
place. A workspace that already had those flows gets a new version of each with the step, which
replaces edits made to them since; orders already running keep the version they started on. On
other flows, add **Apply promotions** after **Price lines**,
and before **Calculate tax** so tax is worked out on the discounted prices. For free shipping, place it after **Shipping rate** too.
The step has no settings: each promotion carries its own rules.

## Combining promotions

Every automatic promotion and every code the order carries is a candidate. Each is checked against
the order; one that doesn't fit is out. Of the rest:

1. They are ranked by priority, higher first, then by what each would take off on its own,
   larger first.
2. The **stackable** ones form one set, applied in that rank order, each on the prices the one
   before left, each held to its own cap. An **exclusive** one is never combined: it is a set of
   one.
3. The set that takes the most off the order wins. An exclusive promotion therefore displaces the
   stackable ones only when it alone takes off more than they do together, caps included. A tie
   goes to the stackable set, then to the exclusive promotion that ranks higher.

Promotions left out are simply not applied. A code left out is told why, naming what it isn't
combined with: `SAVE5 isn't combined with Spring sale, WELCOME10.` An automatic promotion that
doesn't fit or loses is never mentioned, since the buyer never asked for it.

A code that doesn't apply (unknown, paused, expired, for another customer, below its minimum,
covering nothing in the order, used up, or not combined with a better promotion) is left out, and the step's result on the order page says why.
A code reserved for another customer is described exactly like one that doesn't exist, so a buyer
can't discover codes meant for someone else. A
[cart](/guides/carts/) is priced by its flow's steps, Apply promotions included, so a cart's
total is what its order charges.

A promotion counts as used only when an order applies it, and two orders racing for the last use
can't both have it. If the order later fails and rolls back, or is cancelled, its uses are given
back.
