title: Customer accounts
description: Let a shopper sign in on your storefront with an emailed code or link, no password. What they get, what protects it, and what happens to their data.
---

A shopper can sign in on your storefront (`shop.<your domain>`) with an email address and nothing else: no
password to set, forget or leak. Signing in is optional. **Guest checkout stays on**; nobody has to sign in to
buy.

A signed-in shopper is **a verified email address**, not a customer account. Whoever can read the mailbox can
sign in as it, and the orders that address placed are the ones that belong to them. Signing in doesn't create
or change a [customer](/guides/commerce-models/) in your workspace.

:::note
This is the first part of customer accounts. Today a shopper can sign in, see which address they are signed in
as, and sign out. Their orders and saved details come next, and passkeys after that.
:::

## What a shopper does

1. They open **Sign in** on your storefront (`/account/sign-in`) and enter their email address.
2. We email them a **six-digit code** and a **link**. The same email carries both, and either one signs them in.
3. They type the code on the next page, or open the link on the device they want to sign in on and tap **Sign
   in**. The link asks first, and signs in only when they tap, so a mail app or a security scanner that opens
   links ahead of them doesn't use it up.
4. They land on **Your account**, which shows the address they are signed in as and a **Sign out** button.

Every address gets the same answer, whether or not it has ever ordered: signing in never tells anyone which
addresses are your customers.

The pages are plain forms, so they work without script, and they're laid out for a phone and a tablet, in
Safari and installed to the Home Screen. The one exception is the spam check below, which needs script when you
turn it on.

## How long it lasts

- A code and its link work **once**, for **ten minutes**. Using either one ends the other.
- Asking for another code replaces the first: its code and its link stop working. An address can be sent a new
  code once a minute.
- Wrong tries count against the **address**, not the code: five in a window, however many codes were sent, and then
  that address can't be sent a new code for fifteen minutes from its last try. Asking again never gives back tries.
  The link in the email you already have still works while the code is locked.
- An address can be sent at most twenty codes a day.
- A signed-in browser stays signed in for **30 days** from signing in, or until the shopper signs out. Signing out
  ends the session on our side too, not only in the browser.

## What protects it

- **Only we can issue a session.** The code and the link are checked by the engine, which hands the storefront a
  random secret for the browser to keep in a cookie that scripts can't read. We store only a hash of it, so
  reading the database doesn't sign anyone in. Codes are stored as a keyed hash too.
- **A forwarded link can't swap someone's session.** The link's page names the address it signs in (masked, like
  `p***@example.com`) before anything happens, and someone already signed in must confirm replacing their session.
- **Mail is rate limited.** Each address and each visitor has a budget on asking for codes and on trying them,
  and an address gets at most one code a minute.
- **A spam check on asking.** When your workspace administrator has set up a Cloudflare Turnstile widget for the
  deployment, the sign-in form asks for it before anything is sent, so the form can't be used to mail strangers.
  Without a widget the form works without one, but turn it on for any live storefront.
- **Email Sending has to be set up.** Codes go out through the same email sending as
  [order emails](/guides/order-emails/). On a deployment without it, the sign-in page says that signing in isn't
  available.

## Privacy

A shopper's sign-ins and sessions are personal data. An erasure request for their address deletes them and ends
every session they have, and the export for that address lists when they were sent a code and where they are
signed in (never a code or a secret); both are in [data subject requests](/reference/rest-api/#data-subject-requests).
Expired sessions and sign-ins are deleted by the daily clean-up.
