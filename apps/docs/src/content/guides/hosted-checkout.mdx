title: Hosted checkout

A hosted checkout is a checkout for one buyer on your storefront (`shop.<your domain>`), started by
you rather than by the buyer. You make it through the [REST API](/reference/rest-api/#hosted-checkouts), over
[MCP](/reference/mcp/) or with the [assistant](/guides/assistant/) with `create_checkout`, naming the lines
(and the buyer, ship-to and customer if you know them), and you get back an id and a link. Give the buyer
the link; they review the checkout on a page of its own and place the order.

:::note
Card payment is coming. Until it is, a deployment whose payments are only recorded (test mode) shows
a **Place order (test mode)** button that places the order without charging anything, and a deployment
taking real payments shows the page with "Card payment is coming" and no way to place the order.
:::

## Turning it on

Hosted checkout is off by default. While it is off, `create_checkout` makes no link and links already made
stop working. An admin turns it on in the **Hosted checkout** section of the Team page, with
`set_storefront_settings` over [MCP](/reference/mcp/#outside-access) or the [assistant](/guides/assistant/), or with
`POST /api/v1/storefront/settings`, which takes:

- `checkout.enabled`: on or off.
- `checkout.checkoutFlowId`: the published flow that prices every hosted checkout and places its order, or
  `null` for the flow [agentic checkout](/reference/ucp/) uses.
- `checkout.editLines`: whether the buyer may change the quantity of a line, or remove one, on their page.
  Off, only you change what is in a checkout.

Turning it on is refused when the flow that would price it isn't published or doesn't authorize a card
payment, so a checkout can never be made that no flow could take. Changes are confirmed on a card, apply
within about 30 seconds and are recorded in the audit log as `checkout.settings`.
`GET /api/v1/storefront` reads the settings.

## Creating a checkout

```bash
curl -X POST https://app.example.com/api/v1/checkouts \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $COMMERCEWORKER_API_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "lines": [{ "sku": "MON-27", "qty": 1 }], "buyer": { "email": "ada@example.com" } }'
```

The answer is `{ "id": "chk_…", "url": "https://shop.example.com/checkout/start/chk_…/…" }`. The checkout is
priced by your flow, and lasts six hours. The link is shown once and never stored in the clear, so make a
new checkout if it is lost.

## What the link does

The link opens once. Opening it shows a "Continue to checkout" page, so an email scanner or link
preview that fetches the link doesn't use it up; the buyer's tap on the button hands their browser a
cookie for that checkout alone and goes to the checkout. After that, a second visit to the same link, or one after the checkout has expired, is refused and
you make a new one. The cookie is private to the buyer's browser: it opens that one checkout and
nothing else, not another buyer's, not an order, not the admin. A checkout that an AI platform started
over [agentic checkout](/reference/ucp/) is never opened by a hosted link, and the other way round.

## What the buyer sees

One page, which works on a phone, a tablet and a desktop, and without JavaScript:

- **Contact**: email (checked as they send it), first and last name, phone. Browsers fill them in.
- **Delivery**: the address to ship to, the shipping options your flow offers for it with their price and
  time, and where your flow offers pickup, a choice of the stores that hold everything. A checkout of digital
  goods has no delivery.
- **Promo code**: the codes they entered, each shown as applied or refused with the reason.
- **Your order**: the lines, and a total broken into subtotal, discounts, shipping, tax and total, as your
  flow priced them. The page adds nothing up itself, and an order is placed only at the total the buyer saw:
  if the price moved, they are shown the new one first.

Anything wrong is said next to what it is about: an email that isn't one beside the email, a missing
address in Delivery, a code your promotions refused beside the code. What is still to fill in is held back
until the buyer tries to continue. If the checkout changed since the page was drawn (you changed a line,
or they have it open twice), the page says "This checkout changed" and shows it as it is now, instead of
overwriting it.

What the buyer can change is their own details (contact, address, shipping option, pickup store and promotion
codes), and the lines only if you allowed it. They can never change a price. Pages are never cached,
never framed by another site, and put nothing about the buyer in the address.

## Placing the order

In test mode the buyer presses **Place order (test mode)**: the order starts on your checkout flow on the
`web` channel, no card is charged, and they see an order reference. Pressing it twice places one order.
A buyer pays with their own card only: a hosted checkout is never charged to a customer's account or
saved card, even when you made it for that customer.
